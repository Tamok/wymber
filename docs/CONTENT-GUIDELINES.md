# Content & Claims Guidelines

Editorial rules for all user-facing copy — **app UI, marketing, app-store listings, social, and support replies.** The goal: stay clearly a **wellness / self-reflection tool**, never a medical device or therapy. This is both an ethical commitment and the single biggest lever for staying outside FDA/CE medical-device regulation (see `docs/research/2026-05-30-trauma-dbt-brand-research.md` §4).

> Why this matters: a product becomes a regulated medical device when it's *intended to diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate, or prevent* a condition. General-wellness tools that make **no disease claims** generally stay outside active oversight. Claims discipline keeps us there.

## The line
- ✅ We help people **reflect on, organize, and make sense of** their experiences, privately.
- ❌ We do **not** treat, cure, diagnose, or reduce symptoms of any condition; we are not therapy, counseling, or a crisis service.

## Banned (do not use in any claim about the product)
Affirmative medical/efficacy claims — these read as device/treatment claims:

- "treats / treatment for / cures / heals **[a condition]**" (e.g. "treats PTSD", "cures trauma")
- "reduces symptoms / relieves anxiety / lowers depression"
- "clinically proven / scientifically proven / evidence-based **treatment**"
- "therapy / therapeutic treatment / counseling" (as a description of what the product *is*)
- "diagnose / assessment / screening for [a condition]"
- "guaranteed / proven results / works"
- "replace(s) therapy / medication / professional care"
- medical/clinical authority framing ("doctor-recommended", "prescription", "Rx")

## Preferred language
| Instead of… | Use… |
|---|---|
| "treats trauma / heals your PTSD" | "a private space to **map and understand** your experiences" |
| "therapy app" | "self-reflection / wellbeing tool" |
| "clinically proven to reduce anxiety" | "**some people find** mapping helps them notice patterns" |
| "reduces your symptoms" | "supports **reflection** at your own pace" |
| "AI therapist / coach" | "an optional, private reflection prompt" (and only with the §3.5 AI guardrails) |

## Nuance: gentle aspiration is fine; efficacy claims are not
Words like **"healing"**, **"grow"**, **"cope"**, **"safe space"** are acceptable as *gentle, first-person, aspirational* framing ("Your private space for healing"). They become a problem only when phrased as a **product efficacy claim** ("clinically heals trauma"). Keep the subject the *user's own process*, not the product's *effect on a condition*.

## Always pair with
- The persistent **non-therapy disclaimer** (#69) and **crisis resources** (#29).
- First-person, validating, non-directive tone (no "you should", no diagnosing the user).

## Where to apply
App copy · marketing site/decks (`docs/marketing/`) · Apple App Store / Google Play descriptions · social posts · onboarding · email/support.
