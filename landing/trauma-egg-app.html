Is there a trauma egg app?

By JELL, Wymber · Last updated 2026-06-16

Short answer: there is no official "Trauma Egg" app. The Trauma Egg is a clinical exercise from Marilyn Murray's Murray Method, traditionally drawn on paper, often alongside a trained therapist. Wymber is not that, and isn't affiliated with it. What Wymber is: an independent, private, digital tool for mapping your own experiences and their connections, in the same broad spirit as the trauma egg and the trauma timeline, on your own device.

What the trauma egg actually is

The trauma egg is a guided exercise developed by Marilyn Murray as part of her Murray Method. You draw an egg shape and place significant experiences inside it, building a visual record of what happened and how it connects, usually with a clinician guiding the process. It's a structured therapeutic tool, not a self-serve app, and "Trauma Egg" is Murray's own registered approach.

Why people look for a "trauma egg app"

Often it's the wish to do something like the trauma egg, laying experiences out visually to see the connections, but privately, digitally, and at your own pace, without it being a paper sheet someone else holds. That impulse is exactly what trauma mapping is, and it's what Wymber is built for.

How Wymber relates (and what it is not)

Wymber lets you put down dots for the events, feelings, people, places, triggers, coping, and growth that matter, and connect them when a link feels true. It draws on the same lineage as the trauma egg and the trauma timeline. But to be clear about what it isn't:

  • It is not the Murray Method or the Trauma Egg. Those are Marilyn Murray's clinical work; Wymber is an independent tool and is not affiliated with or endorsed by her. If you specifically want the Murray Method, work with a trained practitioner.
  • It is not therapy. Wymber is a self-help tool for personal reflection, not therapy, diagnosis, or a crisis service. It can sit alongside professional care; it doesn't replace it.
  • It does not guide or interpret. Prompts are optional and non-directive; nothing is scored or analyzed. You decide what's true for you.

Private by design (the part an "app" usually gets wrong)

A map of your hardest experiences is about the most sensitive thing you could put in an app, so Wymber is built so it never leaves your device. Your map lives in a single encrypted vault in your browser, sealed with a key only you hold. No account, no servers, no tracking, and the code is open source so that's verifiable rather than a promise. More on why that local-first choice matters for mental-health data.

How to start

Open web.wymber.app, set a password (it encrypts everything on your device), and put down a single dot for something that matters. Add more when you're ready, connect them at your own pace, and step away whenever you need to. Nothing is urgent.

If you're in crisis, you deserve support right now. In the US, call 988 or text 988, or call 911 for emergencies.
