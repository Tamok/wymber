How Wymber encrypts your map (and why we can't read it)

By JELL, Wymber · Last updated 2026-06-12

Short version: your map is encrypted in your own browser with AES-256-GCM, using a key derived from your password. Only the sealed (encrypted) form is ever stored. There is no account, no server copy, and no key anywhere but with you, so "trust us" never enters into it. We couldn't read your map if we wanted to, and neither could anyone who compromised us.

What happens when you create a vault

  1. Your browser generates a random data key (AES-256-GCM), the key that actually seals your map.
  2. Your password is stretched into a wrapping key using PBKDF2 with 600,000 iterations, which makes guessing attacks expensive.
  3. The data key is wrapped (encrypted) by that wrapping key, and separately by a key from your recovery code, so a forgotten password doesn't cost you your map. This pattern is called envelope encryption.
  4. Everything at rest, in your browser's private storage, is ciphertext. Unlocking decrypts the data key into memory for the session; stepping away locks it again.

Why you can verify this instead of believing us

All of it runs on the browser's built-in Web Crypto API (no home-rolled cryptography), and the code is open source under AGPL-3.0: the vault logic is one readable file (crypto.js), and the architecture decision is documented in ADR-0001. A privacy claim you can't audit is just marketing; this one you can check.

What this protects you from, honestly

  • Us, and anyone who breaches us: there is nothing on our side to leak, subpoena, sell, or train AI on. Not as policy, as architecture.
  • A stolen or shared device, while locked: at rest there is only ciphertext; without your password or recovery code it stays that way.
  • Not from everything: someone watching your unlocked screen, malware on your device, or you clearing the browser's site data (which deletes the vault). Honest limits matter; keep your recovery sheet, and an export if your map is precious.

What about sync, or losing my password?

Your recovery sheet (a printable one-time code) reopens the vault if the password is gone. If optional sync ships later, it will be zero-knowledge: only the sealed file would ever travel, and the key would still never leave you. The privacy policy will say so before any such feature exists.
