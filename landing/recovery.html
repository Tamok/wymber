What happens if you forget your Wymber password?

By JELL, Wymber · Last updated 2026-08-09

Short version: your recovery code is the way back in. It's a one-time code shown when you first create your vault. If you saved or printed it somewhere safe, use it on the unlock screen in place of your password, and you're back into your map. If your password is gone and you didn't save the recovery code either, the section below on losing both explains what that means. The answer is not a good one.

What the recovery code is

When you create a vault, Wymber shows you a recovery code once, behind a checkbox you have to tick to confirm you've saved it. It's a long string, 120 bits of randomness written in a plain alphabet (Crockford base32, so no easily-confused characters), and the app never displays it again after that first screen. There's no "view my recovery code" setting to come back to later. If you close that screen without saving it, it's gone, in the sense that no part of the app can show it to you a second time.

It's a second key, not a lesser backup

It's easy to picture a recovery code as a fallback, weaker than the real password, there for emergencies only. Underneath, Wymber generates one random key that actually seals your map, and wraps that same key twice: once with a key derived from your password, and separately with a key derived from your recovery code. Both wrapped copies open the identical map, and neither is a spare. Anyone who has the recovery code can open your vault exactly as if they had your password, in full, with nothing held back.

That has a consequence worth stating plainly: changing your password does not touch the recovery code. Wymber re-wraps the password copy and leaves the recovery copy exactly as it was, still valid, still able to open the same vault. So if a recovery code has been seen by someone you'd rather it hadn't, changing your password afterwards doesn't undo that: the code they saw still opens the vault. There is no action anywhere in the app today that rotates a recovery code on its own. The only way to invalidate one you no longer trust is to start a new vault and move your map into it by hand.

Where to keep it

Paper and a password manager fail in opposite ways, and neither is the right answer for everyone. A printed sheet survives a wiped laptop, a forgotten cloud password, or a device that dies outright, but anyone who can get into the room it's kept in can read it. A password manager survives a house fire and doesn't sit in a drawer, but it depends on its own master password and the device or account it's stored on. For a trauma map in particular, the person most likely to find a printed sheet in a drawer is sometimes exactly the person the map exists to be private from. Only you know which of those two risks, a lost device or a shared home, is the real one for you.

If both are gone

If the password and the recovery code are both lost, the map cannot be recovered, by us or by anyone, permanently. That's the direct result of the design described above, not a missing feature. No key to your vault is held anywhere but with you, so there is nothing on our side to look up, no "verify your identity and we'll reset it" process to run, and no support ticket that ends with your map back in your hands. Building a way around that would mean building a way for someone other than you to get into your map, which is the one thing this design exists to rule out. There's no escrow and no social recovery: the tradeoff that keeps your map unreadable to everyone else is the same one that makes it unreadable to us when the keys are gone.

Clearing your browser, and keeping a copy

Clearing your browser's site data deletes the vault along with it, recovery code or not, since the vault only exists as ciphertext in that storage. If you want a copy that survives a cleared browser or a lost device, export the .wymber file: it stays encrypted wherever you put it, so it's safe to store in a cloud drive or on another disk, but it's only ever as safe as the password (or recovery code) that wraps it.
