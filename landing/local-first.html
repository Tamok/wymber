Local-first: why your mental-health data shouldn't live on someone's server

By JELL, Wymber · Last updated 2026-06-12

Local-first software inverts the usual cloud arrangement: your data lives on your device, and the software comes to the data, instead of your data living on a company's servers where you visit it. The term comes from Ink & Switch's local-first essay, which frames it as a question of ownership: whose data is it, really, if a company can read it, lose it, or lock you out of it?

Why it matters most for mental-health data

A map of your hardest experiences is about as sensitive as data gets. The track record of app companies holding such data is not reassuring: Mozilla's Privacy Not Included reviews of mental-health apps found the category among the worst they'd studied for data sharing and weak policies, and in the US the FTC has had to act against health apps under the Health Breach Notification Rule. The pattern repeats because the incentive exists: collected data can be monetized, subpoenaed, breached, or "anonymized" and shared. The only durable fix is for the data never to be collected.

A policy is a promise; an architecture is a fact

  • A privacy policy can change with an acquisition, a pivot, or a quiet update. An architecture with no server-side data has nothing to change its mind about.
  • A server can be breached. A server that holds nothing can leak nothing.
  • A company can shut down and take your data with it. A local file, in an open format with open code, outlives the company.

How Wymber does local-first

Your map lives in a single encrypted vault in your browser's private storage: no account, no telemetry, no server copy, and the app keeps working offline. The trade-offs are stated plainly rather than hidden: clearing the browser's site data deletes the vault (keep the recovery sheet, and an export if it's precious), and sync across devices doesn't exist yet; when it arrives it will be opt-in and zero-knowledge. The privacy policy is short because the architecture already is the policy.
