What is trauma mapping?

By JELL, Wymber · Last updated 2026-06-12

Trauma mapping means laying out difficult experiences visually: the events, the people and places involved, the feelings and body sensations they left, the triggers that echo them, and the coping, support, and growth that came after, and then drawing the connections between them. Instead of one long story told start to finish, you get a map you can look at, add to, and rearrange at your own pace.

Where it comes from

The idea is older than any app. Therapists have long used paper exercises like the Trauma Egg (Marilyn Murray's Murray Method) and the trauma timeline, where you sketch significant experiences and look at how they relate. Narrative therapy, from Michael White and David Epston's own Dulwich Centre, calls part of this externalizing: putting the story outside yourself, where it's easier to look at, so you are the one examining it rather than the one drowning in it.

Why a map instead of a journal?

  • Connections become visible. A journal is linear; lived experience isn't. A map lets "this trigger traces back to that event" or "this coping skill helps with these two feelings" exist as actual lines you drew.
  • It doesn't demand chronology. You can start anywhere, with whatever feels safe to put down today, and stop whenever you need to.
  • Small pieces are gentler. One dot ("a place", "a feeling") asks much less of you than a blank page asking for the whole story.

How Wymber approaches it

Wymber is a digital descendant of those paper exercises. You put down dots of eleven kinds (events, emotions, body sensations, people, places, triggers, coping, support, needs, insights, growth) and connect them when a link feels true. The design follows SAMHSA's trauma-informed principles: nothing is urgent, nothing is mandatory, suggestions are questions rather than changes, and you can step away at any moment. And because a map like this is among the most sensitive data a person can create, Wymber keeps it on your device, encrypted, with no account and no server copy.

Is trauma mapping therapy?

No. Mapping is a self-help reflection practice, and Wymber is a self-help tool. It can sit alongside professional care (some people bring their map to sessions), but it doesn't replace it. If you're in crisis, you deserve support right now: in the US, call 988 or text 988, or call 911.
